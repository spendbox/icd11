Foundations

What is the ICD?

The International Classification of Diseases (ICD) is the World Health Organization’s global standard for recording, reporting and analysing health information. It turns a clinical story into a structured, shareable code.

🌍

A common language

Every diagnosis maps to a code that means the same thing in Lagos, London or Lima — enabling comparison across the world.

📊

The basis of statistics

Mortality, morbidity, disease burden and the causes our patients present with are all derived from ICD coding.

💳

Operations & funding

Billing, insurance (NHIS/HMO), resource planning and research all depend on accurate, consistent classification.

In one line: If it isn’t classified, it isn’t counted — and what isn’t counted rarely gets funded, staffed or improved.

The new standard

ICD-11 — built for the digital age

ICD-11 came into effect on 1 January 2022. It is the first edition designed to live inside electronic systems rather than on paper, and it changes how we code, not just the codes.

  • Foundation Component — a living digital knowledge base, updated continuously, from which all codes are derived.
  • ~17,000 diagnostic categories and over 55,000 stem codes, expandable to 1.6 million+ codable concepts.
  • Post-coordination & clustering — combine a “stem code” with “extension codes” to capture severity, laterality, cause and more.
  • Built-in API & coding tool — software can query WHO directly; no more paper indexes.
  • New & modernised chapters — e.g. Traditional Medicine, Immune system, Sleep-wake disorders, Conditions related to sexual health.
  • SNOMED CT interoperability — designed to plug into modern EMRs and terminologies.
  • Multilingual — released in multiple languages from day one.
  • Better for primary care & LMICs — includes a linearisation for low-resource settings.

Side by side

ICD-10 vs ICD-11

The leap from ICD-10 to ICD-11 is structural, not cosmetic. Here is what actually changes for a coding clinician.

Dimension ICD-10 (1990, in use since ~1994) ICD-11 (effective 2022)
Design eraPaper-first, manual books & indexesDigital-first, API & database driven
Code format3–7 chars, e.g. B54 (malaria)Alphanumeric 1A00.00–ZZ9Z.ZZ, e.g. 1F40
Detail mechanismMostly pre-coordinated (one code = one idea)Post-coordination & clustering of stem + extension codes
Categories~14,400 codes~17,000 categories → 1.6M+ concepts
UpdatesSlow, periodic revisionsContinuous via the Foundation Component
Chapters22 chapters28 chapters (incl. Traditional Medicine, Sexual health, Immune system)
EMR fitBolted on; limited interoperabilityNative API, SNOMED CT mapping, browser coding tool
SpecificityLimited by fixed structureVery high — severity, laterality, cause captured in one cluster

Example code shift — Malaria: ICD-10 B54 (unspecified)  →  ICD-11 1F40 (P. falciparum) with extension codes for severity.

An honest appraisal

ICD-11: pros & cons

ICD-11 is a major step forward — but adopting it in a busy Nigerian hospital has real friction. Both sides matter.

✓ Advantages

  • Far greater precision through clustering and extension codes.
  • Digital-native — API, coding tool and EMR integration out of the box.
  • Always current — continuous updates, no decade-long waits.
  • Interoperable with SNOMED CT and modern terminologies.
  • Better global comparability of our hospital’s data.
  • Includes Traditional Medicine — relevant to our patients’ reality.

! Challenges

  • Steep learning curve — clustering is unfamiliar to staff trained on ICD-10.
  • Tooling & connectivity — full benefit needs reliable internet/EMR.
  • Granularity overhead — can feel like “too many clicks” at a busy OPD.
  • Migration cost — remapping legacy ICD-10 records and retraining.
  • National adoption lag — NHIS/HMO systems may still expect ICD-10.
  • Local fit — not optimised for our specific disease burden & workflow.
That last point — local fit — is exactly the gap that HCD is designed to close.

Our proposal

Introducing HCD

The Harvey-road Classification of Diseases (HCD) — a lightweight, local classification layer that sits on top of ICD-11, tuned to the realities of Harvey Road General Hospital, Yaba.

HCD is not a replacement for ICD-11. It is a local front-end: every HCD code maps 1-to-1 back to a valid ICD-11 cluster, so we stay globally compatible while coding faster and capturing what actually matters here.

Why does HRGH need its own classification?

🦟

Our disease burden is specific

Malaria, typhoid, sickle cell crises, hypertension and maternal conditions dominate our caseload. HCD puts our top ~50 conditions one tap away.

⚡

Speed at the point of care

Short, memorable codes (e.g. HCD-COM-01 = malaria) reduce clicks and errors in a high-volume OPD.

📍

Capture local context

Extensions for season, RDT vs clinical diagnosis, drug availability and catchment area — data ICD-11 alone won’t surface for us.

🔁

ICD-11 compatible

Each HCD entry stores its ICD-11 stem + extensions, so national/NHIS reporting and global stats are generated automatically.

📈

Drives our decisions

Real-time dashboards of our disease trends inform drug stocking, staffing and outbreak response.

🧑‍⚕️

Built for our staff

Designed around how HRGH clinicians actually document — with training that takes minutes, not weeks.

Under the hood

How an HCD code is built

A single HCD code carries a local chapter, the condition, and optional local modifiers — while silently holding the full ICD-11 cluster underneath.

HCD-COM-01.Rf
HCD — namespace (Harvey-road Classification of Diseases)
COM — chapter: Communicable disease (also NCD, MAT, INJ, MEN, PAE, OTH)
01 — condition index within the chapter (01 = Malaria)
Rf — local modifier: RDT-confirmed, P. falciparum
HCD code HCD-COM-01.Rf Malaria — RDT-confirmed falciparum
maps to →
ICD-11 cluster (auto-generated) 1F40 & XS25 Malaria due to P. falciparum + severity extension
COM · Communicable NCD · Non-communicable MAT · Maternal & reproductive PAE · Paediatric INJ · Injury & trauma MEN · Mental health OTH · Other / symptoms

      Making it real

      Implementation roadmap

      A pragmatic, phased path to bringing HCD & ICD-11 into Harvey Road General Hospital.

      1. Phase 1 · 0–3 months

        Define & map

        Catalogue our top 50 presenting conditions; assign HCD codes; map each to its ICD-11 cluster. Form a small clinical coding committee.

      2. Phase 2 · 3–6 months

        Build into the EMR

        Embed the HCD picker and diagnosis-support engine into the encounter form. Auto-store the ICD-11 cluster behind every HCD entry.

      3. Phase 3 · 6–9 months

        Train & pilot

        Run short bedside training; pilot in OPD and one ward. Audit coding accuracy weekly and refine modifiers.

      4. Phase 4 · 9–12 months

        Scale & report

        Roll out hospital-wide. Launch the live disease-trend dashboard; auto-generate NHIS/national reports from stored ICD-11 codes.

      Balanced view

      HCD — benefits & what to watch

      ✓ Benefits

      • Faster, simpler coding tuned to our caseload.
      • Captures local context ICD-11 alone misses.
      • Stays globally compatible via automatic ICD-11 mapping.
      • Powers real-time, hospital-specific dashboards.
      • Low training burden; high staff adoption.

      ! Watch-outs

      • Needs disciplined governance to keep ICD-11 mappings current.
      • Must avoid “drift” from the WHO standard over time.
      • Requires an owner (coding committee) and periodic audit.
      • Initial mapping effort for the top conditions.
      • Not a substitute for clinician training in core diagnosis.

      From a global standard to a local advantage

      ICD-11 modernises how the world classifies disease. HCD lets Harvey Road General Hospital harness that power on our own terms — faster, local, and still globally compatible.
